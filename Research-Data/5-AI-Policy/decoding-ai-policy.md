## Series Introduction

The "From Compute to Congress" series aims to bridge the language gap between policymakers and security engineers, developing an AI-enabled cybersecurity playbook from the lens of offensive AI security researchers.

## Three Focus Areas

1. **Developing AI-enabled cybersecurity** — Identifying opportunities to automate manual cyber labor through careful federal policy, strategic small business investments, public-private partnerships, and R&D funding.

2. **Strengthening America's national security posture** — AI security feeds into all pillars of the DIME(FIL) model (Diplomatic, Information, Military, Economic, Finance, Intelligence, Law Enforcement). The connective tissue includes rules of engagement in hybrid warfare, information exchange value, and America's opportunity to lead the global AI race.

3. **Supporting the ecosystem** — Democratizing LLMs requires workforce rescaling, energy infrastructure, federal regulatory guidelines (superseding patchwork state policy), and awareness of international regulatory impacts.

## The Offensive AI Normalization Argument

Offensive operations are a critical component of national security. As the White House NSC Senior Director for Cyber noted at RSA 2025: "Not responding is escalatory in its own right. If you continually let the adversary hack you, that's a norm." The use of AI in offense must be normalized and discussed at the policy level.

## The Industry-Government Divide

RSA 2025 observations:
- Government occupied a smaller presence than in prior years
- Most policy talks focused on information sharing difficulties, whistleblowing ethics, and critical infrastructure defense
- As the U.S. government withdraws from industry-led security convenings, big tech fills the vacuum
- With increased AI startup acquisitions, industry carries tremendous weight in policy discussions

## Existing Successes

1. **R&D investments** — Federal funding has enabled significant AI progress, particularly through university-led research
2. **Small business funding** — SBIR/STTR programs have fostered innovation including vulnerability detection in OSS and OT systems
3. **Public-private partnerships** — DIU's Thunderforge project (Scale AI), DARPA's AI Cyber Challenge
4. **Strategic regulations** — CIRCIA (cyber incident reporting) as a forcing function for transparency
