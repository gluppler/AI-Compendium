This module covered the full spectrum of foundational AI concepts, from classical algorithms to modern deep learning architectures.

Supervised learning introduced the core learning paradigm — linear regression, logistic regression, decision trees, naive Bayes, and support vector machines — each suited to different prediction and classification tasks.

Unsupervised learning extended this to unlabeled data, where k-means clustering finds natural groupings and PCA reduces dimensionality by identifying directions of maximum variance.

Reinforcement learning introduced a fundamentally different model: an agent that acts in an environment, receives rewards or penalties, and learns optimal behavior through Q-learning and SARSA.

Deep learning built on all of this — starting with perceptrons and MLPs, then CNNs for spatial data and RNNs for sequential data, with backpropagation and activation functions as the engine driving all of it.

Generative AI closed the module: large language models and diffusion models, their architectures, and the challenges they address.

These foundations provide the vocabulary and conceptual grounding needed to engage with more advanced AI content.
